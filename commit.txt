Measure Step 5 rather than assert it

The demonstration printed O(log d) against O(d^2) as a conclusion with nothing
behind it. `d2048 scaling` now builds the exact monomial at eight dimensions
and counts the bits each representation actually needs.

The claim holds, and it comes with a constant. Across d from 16 to 2^20 the
algebraic width settles at about thirteen bits per bit of d, and it decomposes:
bits(a) = 6*log2(d), bits(b) = 5*log2(d), bits(D) = 2*log2(d). At d = 2048 that
is 145 bits against 536,870,912 for the amplitude list. The ratio is still
falling toward its asymptote at 2^20, which is the shape a log law has and a
constant does not.

The generators are built for arbitrary d rather than pinned at 2047, since a
scaling law cannot be read at one point. The multiplication is checked, so a d
whose product would exceed i128 refuses the row instead of wrapping and
reporting a small number. Above 2^20 that is what happens, and the ceiling is
the arithmetic here, not the method.
