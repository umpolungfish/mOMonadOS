One notation in the dialect table

The first twelve dialects printed their gates from hand-written strings carrying a
second notation: Phi, Phc, Omega, f and Gm standing in for the marks, and the
English names of Shavian letters standing where the letters belong. Every dialect
already carries its gates as specifications in the marks, so the strings were
display only and are deleted. Every dialect prints from its own specification.

A threshold is a value on a mark's axis, so it prints as the letter that value is,
the first value on the axis meeting the bar, rather than as the ordinal number
standing behind it.

The letter names woe, monad, roar, err and haha appeared as words in dialect
descriptions and in the register readouts across four more modules, and the parity
mark had been mangled to an ASCII less-than throughout the descriptions. Both
corrected.
