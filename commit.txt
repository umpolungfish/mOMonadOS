erdos: two more walks, and one that caught me

sumset and ramsey33. R(3,3) = 6 searches all 32768 two-colourings of K6 for a
monochromatic triangle and finds one every time, then shows the pentagon
colouring on five carrying none.

The sumset walk failed twice before it was right, and both failures were mine
rather than the theorem's. The first included 0 in the base-three set, so the
empty subset and {0} collided. The second asserted that the base-three set has
distinct subset sums, which it does not — 1 + 3 = 4 and 4 is in the set. What the
manuscript proves is the implication and the counting bound it forces, so the
walk now runs the paper's own five witnesses: each is progression-free, each has
distinct subset sums, and each satisfies 2^n <= nN + 1.

A step that computes can fail. That is the point of computing it.
