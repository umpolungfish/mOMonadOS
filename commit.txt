The compiler splits over the tied bases and fuses, and the generators are
projected back onto unitarity.

Several braid words sit at the same distance from a target, and the code that
was meant to try each of them lived behind a condition that never held, since
approximate_gate always passes a prebuilt net. Three successive rewrites of that
block produced byte-identical output, which read as convergence and was in fact
dead code. solovay_kitaev_arm now names which tied base a branch takes and
carries it the whole way down so branches stay apart, and sk_split_fuse has the
arms that lost compile the residual left by the arm that won.

projective_distance no longer evaluates sqrt(1 - |tr|/n). That form subtracts
two numbers agreeing to fifteen digits: it carries a few percent of error at
1e-5 and floors to exactly zero below 1e-8, which is inside the range these
braids reach, so it reported perfect gates that were not perfect. Removing the
optimal phase and comparing elementwise never forms that difference.

The generators came out of the F-move sum 1.4e-13 off unitary, flat in strand
count, and the braid words accumulated that at a constant rate per generator
regardless of length. Constant rather than growing is the signature of a defect
at the source, so one Newton step onto the nearest unitary moves every word at
once: generators to 3.3e-16 and per-generator accumulation from 5e-14 to 5e-17.

check_pentagon tested only F^2 = I. It now also checks what the pentagon forces,
real and symmetric with nonvanishing off-diagonal, anti-diagonal, normalized,
and is labelled for what it verifies.

fibqc reaches all of this from the REPL; the module was wired to a boot line and
nothing else. The net build used a linear membership scan, ~1e8 comparisons at
depth 12 behind software floats, and closest_arm walked the net twice per
lookup. Both are single-pass now.
