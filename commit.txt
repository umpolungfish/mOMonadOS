Moved gpu_sixteen3_tensor_kernel's input generation onto the GPU. Once
verification stopped being the bottleneck, host-side RNG (a single-
threaded loop filling three n-length buffers, then htod-copying them) was
89% of total time at n=10^9. Each thread now derives its own x, y, z from
its own index and a seed via a MurmurHash3 finalizer applied to (seed,
counter) -- Lando's suggestion, a hand-rolled counter-based generator in
the kernel instead of cuRAND, since the mixing only needs to look uniform
per-lane, not survive statistical scrutiny as a general-purpose RNG. No
host loop, no htod copy of the inputs at all now; the kernel writes its
generated x, y, z to device memory (so the control check can still read
a slice of them back) but the host never sends them.

Reran the full 10^4-10^9 sweep, kept stage 2's numbers as their own file
(gpu_sixteen3_tensor_kernel_scaling_gpu_verify_host_rng.csv) rather than
overwrite them, alongside the original stage 1 file. Total wall time at
n=10^9: 180ms, down from 6283ms with GPU verify alone, down from 30427ms
in the original CPU-verify version. About 169x cumulative, peaking at
179x at n=3x10^8. Every stage still checked on-GPU against the
independent flat kernel and against a fixed 10,000-entry CPU control
sample: 0 mismatches at every n tried, self-reference check holds.
