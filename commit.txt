Compute the four levels instead of naming them

Step 4 listed sqrt(3), sqrt(5), sqrt(409) and sqrt(683) at levels 12, 5, 409
and 2732 and evaluated none of them. They are evaluated now, on the kernel, by
direct summation of the quadratic Gauss sum g(m) = sum exp(2 pi i k^2 / m),
with k^2 reduced mod m first so the argument stays small and 2732^2 never has
to be representable.

The radical is not the modulus. At a prime level p = 1 mod 4 the sum is real
and IS sqrt(p). At a level divisible by four the sum is 2*sqrt(n)*(1 + i), so
its modulus is sqrt(8n) and the radical is the real part halved. The old check
compared |g(12)| = sqrt(24) against sqrt(3) and ticked it; the modulus was
simply the wrong place to look, and the right place is the component.

Each of the four now reports its sum, its modulus, the radical extracted, which
component it came out of, and the error against the radical it should be:
all four land within 1.5e-13. Their product reconstructs sqrt(D) to 1.3e-11,
and eps = (2047 + product)/2 now comes out of the sums rather than out of a
seeded constant.
