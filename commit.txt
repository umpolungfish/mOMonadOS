ringspec: a ring's spectrum in integers, and what its gap does and does not say

A forged ring's material sheet arrives from the host as decimals — ρ=3.1623,
gap=0.0000 — and a decimal has to be believed. For an integer-weighted ring it
does not: the adjacency is an integer matrix, its characteristic polynomial has
integer coefficients, and every question the sheet answers with a rounding is
answered here by one of those coefficients. Faddeev–LeVerrier, every division
exact, the whole computation in i64.

On the Erdős 3 settled ring, weights 1 2 2 1: det(λI−A) = λ⁴ − 10λ². Symmetric,
so the top eigenvalue is paired with its negative and the gap is 0 exactly. Two
zero eigenvalues, so the spectrum is {0,0,±ρ}, and tr(A²)=20=2Σwᵢ² pins ρ²=10.
Checked against the bare cycles: C₄ gives λ⁴−4λ², C₆ gives (λ²−4)(λ²−1)², C₅ is
asymmetric — all three agree with a numerical diagonalization.

The part worth writing down is what the gap does NOT say. A symmetric spectrum is
exactly bipartiteness, and a cycle is bipartite exactly when it is even, so the
gap is settled by the unit count before a single weight is read. I first wrote
that up as a warning — that a zero gap was "only parity" and the doubling of the
mediating unit had settled nothing. That was wrong in a way worth naming: it set
the count against the structure as rival causes. The ring is even BECAUSE
something mediates on both sides of what it mediates; bipartite means the units
fall into two classes with every bond running between them, and that alternation
is the doubling. One fact, two readings.

So the tool says both: gap 0 exactly, and gap 0 for any weights at this parity.
Compare gap within a parity, strain across.

Also carries the parse the REPL forces on it — splitn(4,' ') delivers "1 2 2 1"
as ["1","2","2 1"], so the tool splits again rather than widening a cap other
commands rely on to keep a trailing phrase whole.
