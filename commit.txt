insert: the one-glyph repair sweep, and the first byte the UART was eating

A word that loses weight is rarely rewritten. It is repaired, and the repair is
usually a single glyph in the right place, so the question "which glyph, where"
is small enough to answer exactly rather than argue about: twelve glyphs at
every position, each candidate walked and asked whether it holds.

`insert <word>` reports every one that does. It counts distinct words, not
distinct sites -- inserting a glyph beside an identical one yields the same word
from two positions, and counting both would report a repair twice.

`insert all` puts the same question to every program the kernel carries. The
words are not typed in; each family hands over its programs and each is rendered
from its own tokens. Of the forty, three hold, thirty-three never fire a clear
at all, three are exposed and repairable, and XXIV_Heartbeat_Paradox is exposed
with no repair whatsoever. That last one is the informative case: it has two
independent clear sites rather than one, and a single glyph can only close one
of them. Insert a gap-opener to close the first and five separate glyphs then
close the second. What the sweep is really counting against is clear sites, and
XXIV is the only program in the kernel with more than one.

Splitting `banked_walk` from `banked_report` is what makes this possible -- the
sweep tests verdicts instead of parsing printed text.

Underneath, a defect the sweep surfaced by being run on metal. Every first
command lost its first character: `banked ...` arrived as `anked ...`. Nothing
in the kernel was reading that byte. `qemu -serial stdio` delivers into the
receive register before the guest ever touches the port, and enabling the FIFO
in `serial::init` discards what is sitting there. Clearing fewer bits does not
help; the enable alone does it. So the byte is read out before FCR is touched
and handed back by `read_byte` in its proper place. Confirmed by probe: the
pending byte was 0x41, the `A` that had been going missing.

Both targets rebuilt, and `insert all` gives identical results hosted and bare
metal.
