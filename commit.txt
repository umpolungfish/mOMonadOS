Circuit: substrate round trips through IMASM, and where identity actually holds

Two circuits, both routed through the twelve-glyph alphabet:

  x86 → IMASM → RNA → IMASM → x86
  RNA → IMASM → x86 → IMASM → wasm → IMASM → AA

A binary cannot return byte-identical. vox's classifier ends in a catch-all, so
distinct instructions carry the same glyph and the substrate map has a fiber and
no inverse. Assume identity on the outer composite and sixty-four codons against
twelve glyphs refutes it outright. What closes is the word: each leg is a
retraction, μ∘δ = id on glyphs, δ∘μ idempotent rather than identity, and the
identity holds exactly on the image of δ.

The RNA leg is enumeration rather than assignment. Four bases give sixteen
ordered pairs at the first two codon positions, four diagonal and twelve not,
and twelve is the size of the alphabet. The off-diagonal pairs are the glyphs.
The third position carries no glyph, which is what rebis/codon.rs already says
about the exact stratum. Diagonal codons are the part of codon space the
alphabet does not reach, and they cannot enter a circuit.

The two machine substrates differ structurally. wasm has structured control
flow, so block and end are opcodes and all twelve glyphs are expressible. x86 is
flat: ⊢ opens a word and ∋ marks a merge, and neither is an instruction. The
lifter re-derives both from the instruction stream, so the word still closes.

Circuit two closes on the canonical section and fails off it, and the failure is
the result. Of four off-section codons in a test sequence, δ∘μ moved all four
and only one changed the protein: GCC→GCU stays Ala, UGC→UGU stays Cys,
ACG→ACU stays Thr, AUG→AUU goes Met to Ile. The detour is invisible exactly
where the code's own third-position degeneracy absorbs the move, and visible
exactly where that degeneracy breaks. Met is a singleton box, so its third
position carries information, and the wobble is what discards it.

Built on the existing rebis codon table and the belnap_ring_shor glyph enum
rather than a second copy of either. REPL command `circuit` with table,
retract, one, two. Findings in CIRCUIT.md.
