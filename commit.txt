prime_winding factor now runs Brent's rho past the trial-division bound
instead of stopping at "search incomplete" the moment TRIAL_DIVISION_BOUND
runs out. Every seed is its own independent walk over the same n -- six
distinct strands, and any one of them closing (a nontrivial gcd) splits
it -- so on a hosted build they run as six real OS threads at once
(std::thread, the same pattern this binary's own live_hud already uses),
racing to the first factor; bare metal, with no std::thread, walks the
same six seeds one at a time. Splits recurse: each half is checked with
is_prime (real Miller-Rabin) and, if still composite, handed back into
the same parallel search, until every piece is confirmed prime or a piece
resists all six seeds within their step budget -- reported as exactly
that, a known composite this search did not finish, never folded into
the prime list.

Building this caught a real bug before it shipped anywhere Lando saw it
work: the first port of Brent's-rho's tortoise/hare structure reset the
tortoise checkpoint to the hare's live position at the start of every
doubling round, instead of holding it fixed for the whole round the way
the actual algorithm requires. It compiled, ran, and returned answers --
and was still wrong: it factored small cases (8051, 10403) by accident
(their factors are close enough that a short local window catches them)
but missed factors a correct implementation finds in tens of thousands of
steps, most of the time, on ordinary 20-24 digit semiprimes. Caught by
building a random 24-digit semiprime with known factors and finding a
2-in-3 failure rate on cases with no reason to be hard, then verifying
the exact same seeds and step budget in an independent Python
reimplementation to confirm it was the algorithm's structure and not a
one-off. The fix holds the tortoise fixed for the whole round, walks the
hare r steps ahead, batches the gcd over groups of 128 steps with a
product-mod-n accumulator, and backtracks one step at a time on the rare
case the batch gcd lands on n itself rather than the real factor --
standard Brent, checked against the textbook shape this time rather than
trusted from the port.
