Built gpu_native_cycle.rs, phase_6 of the GPU-native-build ob3ect: a real
THINK->ACT->OBSERVE->UPDATE tick loop, one glyph of the protocol word per
tick instead of the two undifferentiated passes run/run_real/run_chained
already used. Each tick's ACT is a real device action keyed on the token,
not a log line describing one: VINIT allocates and drops a buffer, TANCH
is a real stream barrier, AFWD/CLINK launch a small per-thread kernel,
FSPLIT launches the divergence kernel and holds the counts, EVALT/EVALF
read the held arms, FFUSE clears them, IFIX increments the append-only
winding invariant. Reports two instruments on the word before running
anything, named separately rather than folded together: tri_ancestral_
word_verdict (the existing gate) and check::word_verdict, the classic
mu-circ-delta closure condition, which nothing here had asked this word
about before. Ran live on the RTX 4070, 22 real ticks.

Asking that second instrument surfaced a real defect in the "repaired"
protocol word this file already shipped: check::word_verdict came back F,
not because of anything about closure, because inserting VINIT (zero
in-arity by its own contract) between ENGAGR and FFUSE gives it a
predecessor, a genuine grammar violation neither of the other two
instruments this file already checked can see. Fixed at the source
(imasm_core::lattice_flow::candidate_holds now requires all three), then
reran the ob3ect's repair search on the original unrepaired word with the
corrected instrument. No single-glyph insertion satisfies all three --
exhaustively checked, 264 candidates, zero survive, a real result, not a
search gap. 19 permutation-based repairs do, at crystal address 16404190
and 16144989, edit distance 2 each. Left PROTOCOL_WORD as it stood pending
a decision on whether a permutation repair -- which moves two existing
glyphs rather than adding one -- is acceptable against the ob3ect's own
phase_4 step-by-step domain_action table, since a swap changes what two
existing step numbers mean where an insertion would not have.
