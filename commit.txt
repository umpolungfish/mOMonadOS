One alphabet: braid_protocol delegates, and Token meets Glyph

A review reported the kernel emitting the retired glyphs and rejecting the
canonical ones. It read momonados-proof-2026-08-02/imasm_core/src/classic.rs and
called that the canonical reference. That path is a frozen vehicle — a copy of
the tree made for emailing, two months stale — and the live imasm_core had
already migrated: code() emits ⋈ ∈ ∋ ⊤ ⊥ ◻ and parse() takes all twelve marks
and says in its own comment that ◇ ● ☊ ☋ are not tokens. Two of the four claims
were about a snapshot. Two were about the kernel, and both were right.

braid_protocol carried its own parser: twelve ASCII names, every glyph refused.
So ⋈ parsed through Token::parse and failed here, two parsers for one type
disagreeing about the alphabet. It delegates now, and token_name delegates too —
its private table answered UNKNOWN for the three-arity and rotation tokens the
type has carried for a while.

And nothing joined Token to Glyph, though both spell the same twelve marks.
belnap_ring_shor now carries the bridge, being the module that owns Glyph. It is
not a bijection and does not pretend to be: FSPLIT3 and FFUSE3 share ∈ and ∋
with the dyad, EVALI shares ⊞ with ENGAGR, ROTAT is ↻ and no axis at all. So
Token → Glyph is partial and many-to-one, Glyph → Token picks the core opcode,
and a glyph word that contains a retired mark fails at the position that holds
it rather than dropping it and quietly shortening the program.

`fibqc tangle ∈ > ∋` and `fibqc tangle FSPLIT AFWD FFUSE` now agree, and
`fibqc tangle ◇ > ●` is refused.

The vehicles stamp themselves NOT_CANONICAL.md from now on, and the two that
already misled a reader are stamped by hand. A snapshot dated in its own
directory name is evidence about that date and nothing else.
