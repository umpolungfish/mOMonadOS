The kernel can cycle a word and account for its weight.

cycle walks a word around its ROTAT orbit. Every rotation is the same object,
so the verdict and the topology hold across it while the final register does
not, and the report ends with the map from cut to landing register. That map is
the whole point: the phase is the only handle on where a word comes to rest.

weight counts what the union throws away. The fork is a set and the fuse a
union, so a finished walk keeps which base values were touched and nothing else.
This records deposits, the clears that empty the register while frames keep what
they banked, and the fuse restoring exactly what a clear destroyed. The lift of
OR to weights is max rather than sum, since adding counts each deposit twice,
once landing in the register and again when its frame closes; at weights zero
and one the accounting reduces to the set semantics exactly.

Two movements carrying no weight are reported because a final register hides
both. SEED, where AFWD or IMSCRIB assigns T to an empty register directly, so a
walk can land in T having carried nothing. INERT, where IFIX has fixed the
register and everything but IFIX and IMSCRIB is a no-op.

The k=11 cut of the worked example is the case: it begins with the fixation, so
eleven of thirteen steps are inert and the T is seeded rather than carried.
Nothing moved because nothing ran.
