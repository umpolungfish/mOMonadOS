Stop the compile while there is heap left to say why

`qc H T X S T T H X X X 13` panicked with "memory allocation of 0 bytes failed" after
reporting a successful compile. Two separate faults.

The bump allocator returned null for a zero-size request. With the arena nearly full the
alignment round-up alone pushes past the end, so a zero-byte allocation failed and the
error path named a size nobody had asked for. A zero-size allocation now returns a
dangling but correctly aligned pointer, which is what it is.

And the depth ceiling of 12 was measured against a short circuit. A ten-gate target at
the same depth builds a 16956-entry net taking 6864 KB of 8192, and the fuse and the
synthesis then run out partway through. The compile now checks headroom after the net is
built and stops while it can still report, naming a lower depth to retry from.

Verified: the failing case reports 866 KB left and suggests depth 10; at depth 10 the
same circuit compiles to error 1.37e-4 with an 11.3x gain, and short circuits at depth 8
are unaffected.
