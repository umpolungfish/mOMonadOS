Built gpu_ipc_no_serialization.rs, the last untouched claim in
gpu_native_build_of_momonados's phase_5: "Direct memory access through the
Crystal address space without serialization." A claim about an absent cost
only means something measured against the cost it's absent of, so this
times both paths on the same real data: SERIALIZED reads IG_catalog.json
off disk and parses it, decoding each entry's 12 Shavian fields with the
same primitive_from_glyph the compiled table's own codegen uses. DIRECT
takes the already-native, already-compiled-in CatalogEntry table straight
to GPU device memory, zero parsing anywhere in that path.

Cross-checking the two paths by name (required before trusting a timing
number at all) found 12 of 8328 matched entries genuinely disagree on
their crystal address. Traced two by hand, clink_l8 and electron: both are
the compiled CatalogEntry table holding an older tuple than what's now
written in IG_catalog.json for that name -- catalog.rs documents itself as
generated FROM the JSON, and these 12 are stale relative to it. Real
divergence between the compiled kernel and its own source, not a decode
bug in this module, and a separate finding from the timing question,
reported as such rather than let it block the measurement.

Ran it repeatedly on the RTX 4070 rather than trust one number: cold-cache
first read costs far more than warm ones (10x on a genuinely cold read,
settling to 3.5-3.8x once the OS page cache is warm), direct path steady
around 10ms, serialized around 36ms warm. The gap is real and is entirely
the disk-read-and-JSON-parse cost the direct path never pays.
