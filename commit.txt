Draw the braid word

qc and jp print braid words as runs of signed integers. That is the exact object and it
is unreadable past a dozen crossings; a compiled circuit runs to hundreds, and what comes
out is a wall of digits in which nothing about the braid is visible. A braid word is a
picture. This draws it.

`bi <generators>` gives a strand diagram in the terminal, three rows per crossing, with
the under-strand absent at the middle row. That break is the whole content of the sign:
without it a crossing and its inverse draw identically and the diagram degrades into a
count. The crossing index sits in the gutter, since the reason to look at a 385-crossing
word is usually to find one crossing in it, and a window like `40:24` draws
twenty-four crossings from the fortieth.

`bi svg` gives the same braid as a file. Over and under are drawn the way they are drawn
on paper: the under strand is laid down whole, then the over strand is stroked twice, once
wide in the background colour and once at width in its own, so the gap follows the curve
without anyone having to split it. Colour follows the strand rather than the position —
the permutation is carried along, so the line leaving the top at position three is the
same colour wherever it arrives at the bottom, and a long word's structure is readable.

Long words fold into columns of 48. Unfolded, the 897-generator word `qc XTT 4` compiles
to is 202 by 27038 pixels: an image in the sense that a filing cabinet is a book. Folded
it is 3118 by 1568, and the shape of the Solovay-Kitaev output is visible in it — long
helices on one pair of strands, then a hand-off to the other. `/N` sets the column height
and `/0` restores the single column.

`qc draw` and `qc svg` render what they compile. The word lives inside the compile's heap
scope and dies with it, so the choice travels in rather than the word travelling out.

Checked against σ1σ2σ1 on three strands: permutation 3 2 1, writhe 3. Checked crossing by
crossing on 1 2 -1 -2 1 2 -1 that the strand drawn whole through each crossing is the one
the sign puts on top, and that the rendered SVG's colours agree with the permutation the
word induces.
