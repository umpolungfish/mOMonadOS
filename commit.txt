Built gpu_sixteen3_tensor_kernel.rs, a fourth GPU ob3ect nobody had touched
this session. Its own phase_4 names a specific shape per gate: split into
a T-arm and F-arm, compute each separately, a real lane permutation on the
F-arm "if required by the gate," rejoin into one register, hold the B
state where the result has both, chain into the next gate, a self-
reference check, commit. gpu_sixteen3.rs already computes the identical
bits as one flat per-lane expression -- correct, but not this shape, and a
structure's shape is its own content, not an artifact of how it happened
to get built. This builds the two-arm split/rejoin explicitly, three real
gates chained through it: union (a combine, no permutation needed) into
meet_t (chained input) into truth_swap (the one of the three that
actually exercises the permutation step, T and F trading places).

Checked against the CPU at every stage, not just the end, plus a genuine
self-reference check: truth_swap applied twice must recover its own
input, verified per-thread on the GPU output, not assumed from the
algebra. Ran live on the RTX 4070 at 5,000,000 register triples: 0
mismatches on all three stages, 0 self-reference failures. The "both T
and F held" counts for stage 2 and stage 3 came out identical, which
they must -- swapping T and F can't change whether a register holds both
-- a structural check the kernel passed for free, not one I had to add.
