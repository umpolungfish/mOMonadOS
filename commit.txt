Vox: claim only what is provably code, and report the rest as unread

The sweep walked every unclaimed byte and decoded whatever fell out, advancing
one byte on a failure. That does not discover functions. It manufactures them at
offsets that are the middle of some other instruction, and the words it produces
verdict like any other, so an invented function is indistinguishable from a real
one in the output.

Which bytes of a stripped binary begin a function is not recoverable from the
bytes. Four things prove it and nothing else does: descent from the entry,
a surviving symbol table, an address the program branches to, and the first byte
after a run of padding. The sweep now starts only at those, walks in phase, and
stops at a terminal.

What is left over is reported rather than guessed. `Walk` carries the claimed
byte count against the image size, and `vox lift` prints it: 12% of /bin/true,
61% of /bin/ls, 88% of /bin/bash. The remainder sits behind an indirect transfer
or is not code, and nothing in the bytes tells those apart.

That is a smaller number than the byte-walk reported and a truer one. The old
sweep put 29,725 instructions on /bin/ls by decoding through data; this puts
11,543 on it and says what it did not read.

Also: every direct branch target seen during descent is recorded even when
descent could not follow it, since a target is proof the bytes at it are code.
