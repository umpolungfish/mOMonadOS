I add combo2: combo (brief) on a word, then weight, banked, insert, and
repair again on every distinct word the first pass's repairs produced. A
repair that closes one rotation's exposure is not yet known to hold on its
own orbit; this is the check for that instead of an assumption of it.

repair.rs gets repair_best_word alongside repair_best_report -- same
cheapest-candidate search, shared through one new cheapest() helper so
the two callers don't run the engine twice; repair_best_word just hands
back the resulting word instead of a formatted line, which is what a
second pass needs to carry forward.

Ran it live on the same 22-letter word: 22 distinct repaired words came out
of the first pass, all 22 audited again in the second, 1398 lines total,
completes clean. It surfaced something real in the process -- several of
those second-pass words read "already holds" from insert while repair's own
search still returns hundreds of candidates with a cost-0, edit-distance-0
permutation as cheapest. Two different questions, not a contradiction: insert
asks whether anything needs adding, repair's broader search still counts
permutations of identical glyphs as candidates even when they change nothing.
