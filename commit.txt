Every tuple came out as >>>>>>>>>>>> — twelve AFWDs — whatever went in. Two
faults, one on top of the other, and both are the same mistake made twice: a
positive combination cannot move an argmax.

aggregate_votes summed all twelve family rows into one score vector before
anything was chosen. Every entry of FAMILY_TOKEN_AFFINITY is non-negative, so
the winner of that sum is just the heaviest COLUMN of the matrix — AFWD and
IMSCRIB tie at 11 and AFWD sorts first — and the tuple, which only reweights
rows, could never reach it. build_program_from_scores then recomputed the same
argmax at all twelve positions with nothing positional in the choice, and
AFWD is depth-neutral so no guard ever excluded it. Now each slot is ranked by
its own axis's row, so the twelve axes stay distinguishable, which is the whole
content of a twelve-mark word.

Underneath that, the variant was inert. Scaling a row by the ordinal is
order-preserving: it cannot reorder the row it scales, so it cannot change which
token wins. Slot 8's row is [0,…,0,2,2,2,0] with EVALT, EVALF and ENGAGR tied,
and swapping haha (3.0) for monad (2.0) scaled all three equally and left the
entire word unchanged — likewise ah for zoo at slot 11. The ordinal now steps
down the ranked candidates instead of shouting louder at them: the axis names
which tokens are in play, the value says which of them this is. Every
single-axis substitution moves the word now, including those two.

Still open, and it is the more important one: write and derive are not inverse.
Writing a tuple and deriving the word back gives a different tuple, and the boot
line has been reporting that as hamming=3, weighted=3.5 all along. A measured
gap, not a silent one — but if write is δ and derive is μ then μ∘δ=id is the
thing the pair is for, and it does not hold yet.
