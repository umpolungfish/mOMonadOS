The gate net stops storing every word twice, and depth 12 fits.

The build cloned each level into `entries` and then kept the same vectors as the
frontier, so every braid word was live in two places at once. On a bump
allocator that reclaims only in LIFO order that duplicate is what put the deeper
nets out of reach. The frontier is now a range of indices into `entries`: the
depth-10 net drops from 3553 KB to 1704 KB and the compile peak from 4451 KB to
2602 KB, with output bit-identical.

Depth 12 now runs in-kernel. It costs 6864 KB and peaks at 8156 KB of the 8192
KB arena, a margin of 36 KB, so it is the hard ceiling rather than the practical
one. The figures previously recorded in the README and the help text were host
estimates assuming ideal packing and were low by roughly a factor of three;
they are replaced by measurements.

`fibqc` scopes itself with heap_mark/heap_reset and reports its own high-water
mark. Running out returned null and took the kernel down without printing
anything, which is what the silent fifteen-minute deaths were.

The result this makes reachable: T·S gains 25.4x at depth 12, from 1.978e-4 to
7.792e-6. At depth 10 the same row gains nothing at all. The fuse needs enough
dictionary to compile the surviving arm's residual, and 4842 entries do not
supply it, so the flat row at depth 10 was dictionary-limited rather than
converged.
