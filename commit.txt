Two panels, because the geometry does not fit in one

The figure showed the two shells as tangent circles AND the trine spread at 120
degrees. Those are facts about different planes. The shells appear tangent only
in a plane containing the syzygy axis; the trine stands at 120 degrees only in
the plane perpendicular to it. Drawing both put two mutually exclusive viewpoints
in one picture, which is a perspective that does not exist. plotter3 emits
separate views for exactly this reason and this now follows it.

Left is side-on: the FSPLIT shell centred on IMSCRIB, the evaluator sphere of
equal radius one radius along the axis so IMSCRIB lies on it, FFUSE at the
antipodal pole, and the trine drawn as the vertical span it actually is in this
plane rather than as a triangle it is not. The program rides this panel.

Right is face-on down the axis: the same evaluator sphere as one circle, the
three arms at 120 degrees, the A2 trine, IMSCRIB at the centre.

Both panels are at one scale, so the two readings of the same sphere can be
compared rather than only believed.
