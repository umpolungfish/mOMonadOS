Circuit: strands and frames, and the antisense triple

`circuit rc` prints the sense word, the antisense word, and all three reading
frames of an RNA string.

The finding worth keeping: the antisense of a canonical-section word is forced
to a three-glyph vocabulary, and the three are fork, fuse, and self-reference.
Reverse complement sends (p1,p2,p3) to (comp p3, comp p2, comp p1), so the
antisense strand reads its FIRST position off the sense strand's wobble. With
the canonical wobble at U and comp(U)=A, every antisense codon of a section word
begins with A, and the only glyphs with a leading A are ∈, ∋, ⊙. Twelve
collapse to three. Whatever the sense strand discards is exactly what the
antisense strand puts in a glyph-bearing position.

This also settles that the canonical wobble is not an inert choice: wobble G
would give every antisense codon a leading C and hence the triple <, ⋈, ⊤. The
choice selects which triple the other strand speaks, so it wants a reason rather
than a default. Recorded as the open question it now is.

Sequences run, all reproducible from the REPL:

- Shine-Dalgarno AGGAGG carries ⊙ and nothing else on the message strand and
  nothing at all on the other. Its recognizer CCUCCU carries the mirror. The
  recognition is one strand speaking ⊙ into a strand that says nothing.
- The palindromic site GAAUUC has sense and antisense words that coincide, since
  a reverse-complement palindrome is its own antisense. It lands on ◻.
- Poly-A is mute in every frame on both strands: AAA is diagonal and so is UUU,
  the one case where both sides of the involution sit on the diagonal.
- The telomere repeat UUAGGG is mute in frame 0 and alternates ⊙⊥ without
  interruption in frame 2.
- The hammerhead ribozyme core carries a non-repeating word on both strands.
