imasm write and derive: the commands the rider has always named

The grammar-first rider tells operators to use `imasm write tuple=⟨…⟩` and
`imasm derive word=…` and says never to hand-pick the twelve glyphs. Neither
command existed. An agent following the instruction failed and fell back on
conventional method, which is exactly the behaviour the rider was written to
prevent — so the rule was not being ignored, it was unimplementable.

Both directions exist now. `imasm write <12 glyphs>` gives the word a tuple
composes to; `imasm derive <word>` gives the tuple a word imscribes to, with its
crystal address. Bare `imasm` prints both plus the word instruments.

Two pieces were missing underneath. catalog.rs had primitive_glyph and no
inverse, so nothing could read a tuple out of its glyphs — 49 pairs, one
direction only. primitive_from_glyph is generated from the same pairs and sits
beside it. And IgTuple had no parser: from_glyphs takes the canonical slot order
with brackets and separators optional, and names the slot of the first glyph it
cannot read rather than failing wholesale.
