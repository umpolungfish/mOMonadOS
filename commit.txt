Vox: refuse to score an ill-formed word, and stop calling a section a function

Two things ported back from the standalone V⊙x in ~/imsgct/Vox, where they were
found.

verdict gains F. T closes, B holds a fork open across a terminal, N never
forked, and F is not a truth value at all: it reports that the word is
ill-typed, a fuse with no fork to pair. Scoring such a word T or N buries the
finding under something that looks like an answer. The engine refusing is the
correct behaviour, and it is how both defects below were found rather than
guessed at.

split_functions cuts a decoded run into functions. A contiguous run is not one
function; it is every function laid end to end. A boundary is a terminal, then
padding, then a body, and all three are needed: padding alone is not a boundary
because both compilers also pad for alignment inside a body, and cutting there
shatters one function into dozens. A stretch that is only padding is not a
function either.

`vox lift` now reports per-function verdicts and a tally instead of verdicting
whole sections, and prints the ill-typed words with their addresses.

One difference from the Python lane, recorded rather than smoothed. That lane
walks by recursive descent, so its ill-typed words are functions cut in the
wrong place. This lane lifts linearly, so its ill-typed words are dominated by
loop headers: a back-edge puts the fuse before the fork in address order, and a
linear reading meets the ∋ with nothing on the stack. Same refusal, two
different causes, and the second is a property of reading linearly rather than
of the program.
