Moved gpu_sixteen3_tensor_kernel's verification onto the GPU. The
single-threaded CPU loop was 30x the cost of the GPU work it checked at
n=10^9. Every thread now also computes the same three chained stages via
an independently-written flat kernel (the exact expressions
gpu_native_protocol.rs's CHAINED_SRC already verified separately at
billion scale) and folds any disagreement into one atomic counter per
stage, all O(N) work staying on the device. Two GPU kernels agreeing with
each other isn't the same as being right if they share a bug, so a fixed
10,000-entry sample, regardless of n, still gets checked directly against
the real CPU imasm_core functions as the ground-truth control a
GPU-only self-check can't be. Only seven u64 counters and that small
sample cross back to the host now, not four full n-length arrays.

Reran the full 10^4-10^9 sweep and kept the old numbers rather than
overwrite them: measurements/gpu_sixteen3_tensor_kernel_scaling_before_
gpu_verify.csv is what the module used to measure,
gpu_sixteen3_tensor_kernel_scaling.csv is what it measures now. Total wall
time drops about 5x from 10^6 triples up (5.3x at 10^8), and regresses
below 10^5 -- atomic-counter overhead with nothing to amortize it against,
kept in the chart rather than cropped. Host-side RNG generation of the
input triples is the new bottleneck at scale, not verification: 5.6s of
the 6.3s total at n=10^9.
