The Solovay-Kitaev recursion now fits the arena it runs in

`qc hx 3 7` took the kernel down inside an allocation. The gate net at depth 3
is small and the report said so, a little over a megabyte of a 48 MB arena, and
then the compile asked for 131 KB with 88 KB left. Everything in between had
gone into the recursion, and none of it into anything that survived.

The recursion is three-way, and the word a level returns is five sub-words long,
so the tree is 3^d nodes and the words carry a factor of five per level. That
much is inherent. What was not inherent is that every one of those nodes kept
its sub-words for the rest of the run. The arena is a bump allocator and
reclaims only in LIFO order, so a node that allocates three sub-words and then
builds a fourth out of them cannot give the three back by dropping them. Sixteen
of these runs happen per compile, eight arms and eight fuse passes, and each was
leaking the whole of its own tree. On top of that the level's word was grown by
five extends from a clone, which reallocates four times, and on a bump allocator
each of those intermediate copies is also kept forever.

The fix is that a level collapses to its result before returning. Its sub-words
are dead the moment it has the word built out of them, its word is the topmost
live block, so the word is moved down to where the level started and the bump
pointer follows it. Peak use then tracks the path down the recursion rather than
the whole tree. With the word built in one allocation of the size it will be,
`qc hx 3 7` peaks at 3.8 MB where it used to run 48 MB out, and `qc hx 3 9`,
which is a word of 1.8 million generators reaching an error near 4e-10, peaks at
18 MB and returns.

Compaction has a trap in it that I nearly walked into. The allocator reclaims a
block when it ends exactly where the bump pointer stands, and after a compaction
that is where the result ends. So dropping a dead sub-word of the same length,
sitting at the address the result was just moved to, frees the result out from
under its owner and the next allocation writes over it. It is reachable: when
the net's nearest approximation to both commutator factors is the identity, the
level's word is its base word byte for byte. The dead words are handed back to
the arena scope now rather than to the allocator, which is what the reset was
going to reclaim anyway.

Two more things were eating the arena for nothing. The word check resynthesized
the printed word by building a fresh dense matrix per generator, which on a
million-generator word costs more than the compile that produced it, and that is
what took depth 9 down even after the recursion fit. The fusion space on four
strands is two-dimensional, which is why the compiler works in `Matrix2`
throughout, so the check now folds the generators through registers and costs
nothing. The integer listing was formatting each generator into a throwaway
string, one arena allocation per generator, and writes into the line directly
now.

Sweeping the recursion depth on a small net says where it stops paying, and the
answer is sharper than I expected. Recursion 7 gives 6.4e-8, 8 gives 2.7e-9, 9
gives 3.9e-10, and 10 and 11 give 3.9e-10: the same error, the same word to the
generator, the same high-water mark to the kilobyte. The levels above 9 do not
trade badly, they contribute nothing, and the free reduction is what makes that
visible. A level appends its two commutator factors and their inverses around the
base word, and once the residual falls below what the net can resolve those four
cancel completely and the level returns its own base byte for byte. Without the
reduction the same level would return a word five times longer for no accuracy at
all, and the level above it twenty-five times, and the arena would be what stopped
it. That also means the case the compaction has to be careful about, a level whose
word is exactly its base, is not a corner: past the useful depth it is every call.

Depth is capped nowhere. The net already grew until the arena said stop; the
recursion now asks the same question a level at a time. The size of the word a
level will return is known before any of it is allocated, so a level that will
not fit is declined at the point where the level below it is already a complete
answer, and the run reports the depth it reached instead of the depth it was
asked for. Free reduction of the words at every level came along with this: the
concatenation is where cancelling pairs are created, four seams per level, each
of which the level above would copy five more times. It leaves the unitary
exactly where it was, so the errors are unchanged and only the lengths come
down. `T S` at the defaults still reports 7.260095e-4, in 1068 generators rather
than 1094.

The user guide's compile section had the arena at 8 MB and a hard ceiling of 12
that neither depth has any more, and said nothing about the second depth at all.
It now describes both, and what each does when the arena runs short.

The closed form now draws the closure it is named for. `loop` was laying the
crossings out in horizontal lanes that alternated direction, running the return
path around the outside as a dashed line and leaving the strands of a crossing
joined to nothing, so what came out was neither the braid nor its closure. The
whole thing is redrawn as an annulus, which is what closing a braid is: bend the
page into a cylinder so every strand's foot meets its own head, and look at the
cylinder end on. Strand positions become concentric tracks, one crossing owns one
angular slot, and reading once around the ring is reading the word once through.
Nothing has to be added to close it, because a circle is already closed. The
strands of a permutation cycle join into one curve on their own and each such
curve is one component of the link, which is why colour there follows the
component and not the strand: a component that changed colour where two of its
strands meet would be one closed curve drawn as several. The hole in the middle
is the braid axis, and the caption sits in it.

The curves are the part I would have got wrong by eye. A crossing interpolates
its two radii with a smoothstep in the angle, whose derivative vanishes at both
ends, and every cubic is fitted to that curve's own position and derivative at
its ends rather than to a guessed control point. So a strand leaves its track
along the track, rejoins the next one the same way, and the seam between the
crossing and the arcs either side of it is not there to find. The crossing takes
the middle of its slot and the strands run parallel into and out of it, more of
the slot the fewer crossings there are: swinging across the whole slot draws a
ring of waves in which a crossing is not a crossing to look at.

The ring grows with the crossing count instead of packing them tighter, so a
crossing is the same size to read whatever the length, and the annotation scales
with the figure rather than staying at the size that suited a small one. The file
carries its own stylesheet and answers either theme, which matters more here than
it sounds: the gap in an under strand is cut by overstroking it in the background
colour, so a dark-theme viewer was being shown gaps filled with white. The
palette is named in classes, with a second set lifted for dark.

Also in here, from the same sitting: `braid_protocol.rs`, which carries the twelve
IMASM tokens onto the tangle generators so a program can be read as a braid word
and its invariants taken, reachable as `fibqc protocol`; the `loop` and `curve`
spellings on `bi` and on `qc`; and a hosted-only `svg_out` that writes an SVG to
`ob3ects/` rather than only to the serial line, a no-op on bare metal where there
is nowhere to write it.

It also would not draw a long word. Formatting each element into a string of its
own and pushing that costs an allocation per element and leaves the growth of
every one of them behind, so a drawing well inside the budget it had measured for
itself ran the kernel out of heap two thirds of the way through. It writes into
one reserved buffer now, with three scratch strings for the crossing legs that
are cleared rather than dropped, and a word too long for the arena to hold the
whole document is drawn as far as it fits and says so, in the caption and in the
`desc`, as the closure of the crossings it drew. A forty-thousand generator word
draws twenty thousand of them and comes back.
