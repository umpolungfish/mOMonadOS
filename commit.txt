Finish the mark migration, where a dropped glyph was passing as a proof

The twelve marks are ⊢ ⊣ ≻ ≺ ⋈ ⊤ ∈ ∋ ⊙ ⊥ ⊞ ◻, and imasm_core emits them. Six
places in the kernel still spelled AFWD and AREV as ASCII > and <, and two of
them were load-bearing rather than commentary.

parse_glyph_word is a filter_map over the twelve. It does not reject a retired
mark, it DROPS it, deliberately and by design — a word containing one reads it as
nothing. So the self-application check in parasm, whose word was ⊢∈>⊤<⊥∋◻⊣,
parsed to seven tokens where it names nine: both work opcodes silently vanished,
and the closure it demonstrates was being demonstrated on a shorter word than the
one written down. Its alphabet string carried the same spelling, which is the
only reason the ordinal lookup did not panic — two stale copies agreeing with
each other and with nothing else.

sk_forge's BIP39_DERIVATION_WORD was the same defect. That is the one the last
operator session spent nearly two hundred windings circling, and it was going to
fix it the wrong way round: imasm's printed help still showed > and <, so the
agent read the print as authority over catalog.rs and set out to convert the
word to ASCII. The print was the stale thing.

Fixed at all six: parasm (word and alphabet, plus the comment above them),
sk_forge, imas_ig, circuit, triple_frame, bip39_sic_grover. The word now resolves
to nine ordinals against the alphabet. Release build green.
