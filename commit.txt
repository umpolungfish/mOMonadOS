Lando asked to show algebraic (true quantum) advantage -- the case
neither DQI (turned out classical) nor the YZ separation (real, but
structureless by the paper's own title) actually is. Checked what
already existed in the codebase for this before writing anything new.

Traced belnap_shor.rs's header claim by hand first: "period r is encoded
in the 2:1 coherence cost ratio." The register always starts classical
(all-T), Hadamard costs 1 per qubit regardless of content, B-bias
measurement costs 2 per qubit, T-bias costs 1 per qubit -- so
b_meas_only=2n and t_meas_only=n for any n, any a, any N, giving
ratio=2n/n=2.0 identically. The actual period is computed separately by
a classical brute-force walk (classical_period / count_distinct_outputs)
and reported next to that unrelated constant. Not building on it.
qft.rs only builds gate-count circuit structure, no amplitudes.
period_finding_ecdlp.rs takes the measured phase as a given input and
does real, correct classical post-processing on it, but never derives
that phase from a simulated quantum state.

Added shor_sim.rs: the missing half, a real complex-amplitude simulation
of Shor's period-finding circuit. Uniform superposition plus modular
exponentiation as a basis-state permutation plus a real projective
measurement collapse onto one output branch has one exact closed form --
equal amplitude on every index x whose a^x mod N lands on the observed
value, zero elsewhere -- computed directly rather than gate by gate.
Applied the QFT as the exact unitary DFT on what's left (reused
fibonacci_qc::Complex rather than writing a second complex-number type).
Read the resulting probability distribution, extracted peaks, ran
continued fractions, recovered the period, factored N via gcd.

First run showed the real physics working: for a=7, N=15 (period 4,
register 256), the four peaks landed at k=0,64,128,192 -- exactly
2^8/4 apart, k/M*r landing on exact integers. Genuine interference,
not asserted.

But continued-fraction extraction failed on every one of the four
textbook cases except one. Two real bugs, both caught by reading the
actual output rather than trusting the code:

1. top_peaks.first() picks whichever peak sorts first among exact ties.
   k=0 is always a peak (every periodic comb has a zero-frequency
   component) and always uninformative -- its convergent is the trivial
   0/1. With several peaks tied at the same probability, k=0 often sorted
   first, handing the extractor the one peak with no period information
   in it. Fixed by trying every top peak in turn, skipping k=0, instead
   of stopping at whichever peak happened to sort first.

2. The convergents() function's seed values for the continued-fraction
   recurrence had p and q each set to their own two values swapped.
   Traced convergents(64, 256) by hand against the standard recurrence
   (p_{-2}=0, p_{-1}=1, q_{-2}=1, q_{-1}=0): the correct sequence is
   (0,1) then (1,4), matching k/m=64/256=1/4 exactly. The code as
   written produced (1,0) then (4,1) -- every convergent's reciprocal.
   Fixed the seed values; confirmed the fix against that exact case.

With both fixed, all four classic textbook cases (a=7,N=15; a=2,N=21;
a=2,N=35; a=8,N=21) go the full distance: correct period extracted,
correct factors recovered, verified by direct multiplication against N.
Also ran N=33 (period found, factoring declined on a genuine gcd
degeneracy -- a real, expected outcome of the actual algorithm, not a
bug) and N=91 (full success), and pushed to the 14-qubit cap (register
16384) to confirm it still completes in a few seconds.

Wired as `shor-sim run <a> <N> <qubits>`. Capped at 14 qubits since the
QFT here is a direct O(M^2) unitary matrix application -- exponential in
the qubit count, which is exactly why a real quantum device would have
genuine advantage at a scale this simulation cannot reach; the small
scale is what makes it checkable at all, not a limitation being hidden.
Ported to G-mOMonadOS and reran two of the cases there, identical
output.
