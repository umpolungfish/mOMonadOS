Expose the SK recursion depth, and guard only where it is safe

`qc` swept over net depth showed error that was not monotone: a bigger net gave a worse
answer, twice. The net is a breadth-first append, so net(d) is a prefix of net(d+1), and
`closest_arm` is an exhaustive scan of every entry — so neither a non-nested net nor a
greedy lookup can be the cause.

The recursion depth was pinned at 3 and invisible, which is what made the sweep
unreadable: the reported error is the output of a three-level Solovay-Kitaev, not the
net's own nearest neighbour, and only the latter is monotone in net size. It is a second
argument now, so `qc HTSX 10 0` asks the net directly. Swept that way the curve never
rises, at any step, which locates the non-monotonicity in the recursion and nowhere else.

The mechanism is intrinsic. A level takes its base from the level below, forms the
residual target·g_u⁻¹, decomposes it as a group commutator and approximates the two
factors in the same net. Those factors are unrelated to the target, so how well the net
approximates them says nothing about how well it approximated the target, and a better
base can compose into a worse whole. Every lookup being optimal does not make the
composition monotone.

Declining a level that made things worse looks free, since the base is already computed
and already carries its word. Inside the recursion it is not free: the level above then
builds on a different base, and over the same sweep that made the two best rows five and
six times worse. The comparison is only sound where nothing is built on top of it, so it
happens at the outermost call and nowhere else. There it cannot lose — the result is the
better of exactly what the function returned before and the base it came from.

Measured over the sweep it fires once, at depth 2, same error with the word down from ten
generators to two. Every other row is unchanged, which is the guarantee showing up as
data. It does not make the net-depth curve monotone and nothing cheap will: that would
take keeping the best across several depths, which is post-selection rather than a better
search.
